# JD Decode

Convert the user's job description into an interview-preparation brief. The user should be able to answer three questions after reading it: what this role does, what it requires, and what to prepare first.

## Workflow

1. Work from the supplied JD only. Treat a job title without responsibilities or requirements as insufficient input.
2. Separate the result into:
   - core work and expected outcomes;
   - explicit requirements;
   - bonus items;
   - details to confirm with the recruiter or interviewer;
   - a prioritised preparation checklist.
3. Lead with the result the user needs to act on: the role's focus and the P0 preparation items. Keep the main body concise, then collect each cited JD sentence once in a numbered source index at the end. Cite sources inline as `[S1]`, `[S2]`; do not repeat long JD sentences beside every conclusion.
4. Keep interpretation separate from the source. Label an inference and explain its basis; do not turn an inference into a requirement.
5. Make checklist actions concrete: name the project evidence, knowledge area, or question to prepare, and state what a finished preparation item looks like. Include at least one P0 item when the JD contains substantive work or requirements.

## Boundaries

- Do not score the candidate or request a resume. Resume comparison belongs to a resume–JD matching skill.
- Do not invent the company's business goals, team structure, KPIs, tools, or interview format. Put missing information in the confirmation section.
- If the JD contains only a role name or fewer than two substantive statements, ask for the full responsibilities and requirements before analysing it.
